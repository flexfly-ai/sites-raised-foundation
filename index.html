Know the Warning Signs

Is Your Home Trying to Tell You Something?

Older homes on raised foundations show predictable stress signals. Catch them early and a repair costs a fraction of what it will later. Here's what to look for.

Wall Cracks

Vertical and horizontal cracks in the stem wall indicate foundation movement — not just settling.

Sticking Doors

Doors that drag or refuse to close properly signal that the frame has shifted out of square.

Sticking Windows

Windows that jam or no longer seat flush in their frames are an early indicator of foundation movement.

Squishy Floors

Soft, springy, or noticeably uneven floors point to failing posts or girders beneath your home.

Cracks Above Openings

Diagonal cracks above doors and windows are caused by lateral soil pressure on the foundation.

Common Questions

Foundation Repair — Straight Answers

No jargon. No pressure. Just the information you need to understand what's happening under your home.

Homes built before 1960 in San Diego and Orange County typically have raised foundations — also called crawl spaces. These consist of concrete piers and wooden components like posts, girders, and joists that sit above the ground. Over decades, these components can deteriorate and become structurally unstable.
The expansive and shifting soil common throughout San Diego and Orange County creates lateral pressure on your home's foundation. As the foundation moves, cracks form along the path of least resistance — typically the diagonal corners above door and window openings.
Foundation movement is one of the most common causes of sticking doors and windows across San Diego and Orange County homes. When the foundation shifts, the structural frames around openings shift with it — pulling them out of true so doors and windows no longer seat properly.
Expansive soil conditions cause separation in foundation components over time. When posts or girders fail or sink unevenly, the floor structure above loses uniform support — leading to visible sag, bounce, or slope across the floor surface.
Stem wall deterioration in Southern California homes is typically caused by water damage, cumulative seismic activity over decades, or a combination of both. The good news: early intervention prevents a structural problem from becoming a rebuild.
Older homes with expansive or shifting soil can experience foundation sinking or component failure that robs the floor of its rigid support. The result is a soft, springy feel underfoot — a reliable sign that the structure below needs attention.
Our inspector will examine your home's raised foundation for the seven most common signs of structural problems. You'll get a clear assessment — what's wrong, why it happened, and what repair options are available. No obligation, no sales pressure.
Depending on the severity and location of the damage, options range from targeted partial repairs — replacing specific posts, girders, or joists — to hydraulic leveling or full foundation rebuilds. Our inspection will match the right solution to your actual situation.
Chimney separation is typically caused by earthquakes, water intrusion, or soil shift moving foundation components over time. Because chimneys sit on their own footing, differential settlement between the chimney and the main foundation opens a visible gap.
Bulging stucco or plaster can signal excess water damage — water adds weight to the stem wall and accelerates structural breakdown. Caught early, it's a manageable repair. Left alone, it compounds into severe foundation damage.
